## 2. Why I am a strong candidate beyond the technical match

First, **I know how to partner with academic and research communities**. During my ten years at the University of Oslo, technology was never the final objective; research was. I worked directly with faculty, researchers, PhD students, and laboratory users to understand their work, anticipate needs, translate scientific requirements into technical decisions, explain constraints and trade-offs clearly, and develop appropriate solutions. That **researcher-centered partnership** is directly relevant to DKU.

My approach to architecture is **fit for purpose, not technology for technology's sake**. I do not choose a platform simply because it is the newest option on the market. I consider the research objective, the users' technical experience, software compatibility, reproducibility, reliability, security, available resources, and the long-term support burden. The right architecture is the one that helps researchers make progress and that the IT team can operate responsibly.

At the same time, fit for purpose does not mean standing still. I explore innovative transformations when they can improve research efficiency. For example, bioinformatics researchers needed different tools on the same server, but incompatible libraries made those environments difficult to maintain. I introduced **containers to isolate software environments and reduce library conflicts**, then taught researchers the fundamentals and helped them adopt the approach. This reflects how I work: **partner first, select the appropriate technology, explain it clearly, and help users succeed with it**.
