## 2. Why I am a strong candidate beyond the technical match

First, I understand that platform engineering is not only about running tools. A good platform must improve the daily work of developers and product teams while staying reliable, observable, secure, and maintainable. At Appear, I work on internal platform capabilities that support engineering productivity and production reliability, including Kubernetes operations, GitOps, CI/CD automation, observability, storage, security policy, ingress migration, and Terraform-managed infrastructure.

One example is the production ingress controller migration from NGINX to Traefik. The cluster had hundreds of ingress resources and a complex setup involving ExternalDNS, MetalLB, DNS records, load balancer behaviour, TLS, and rollback requirements. My approach was to design a migration plan, validate it first in a test cluster, adjust the plan based on real findings, and then migrate production in controlled stages. This is how I prefer to work: understand the risk, test assumptions, communicate clearly, and execute carefully.

Second, I bring a practical mindset to emerging AI platform work. I have helped deliver production GPU worker-node capabilities used by developers, while keeping the internal AI/LLM platform clearly scoped as an MVP. That distinction matters to me. I do not present experiments as finished production services. I use MVPs to learn what is operationally realistic, what governance is required, and what should be standardised before a capability becomes a shared platform service.

Third, I am comfortable working across both infrastructure and software boundaries. For example, I led an internal ClickTime and Visma integration even though software development was not my core platform role. I used Rust and AI-assisted development, worked with HR, Finance, project owners, and security stakeholders, and treated the work like a compact software delivery lifecycle: requirements, API analysis, risk review, testing, container packaging, Kubernetes deployment planning, and operational ownership. This experience strengthened how I communicate with non-technical stakeholders and translate broad business needs into controlled technical scope.

Finally, my career has been built through steady growth. At the University of Oslo, I started as a System Administrator, grew into a System Engineer, and then became a Senior Engineer, taking on broader responsibility for Linux systems, data center operations, networking, storage, HPC/GPU support, scientific software, monitoring, and research users. Later, at Sopra Steria and Appear, I moved further into enterprise automation, Kubernetes, DevOps, platform engineering, and security governance. This path has made me both hands-on and careful about long-term operations.
